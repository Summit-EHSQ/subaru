# ADR-099: Model Pilot Execution Through Shared Shipments and Event-Scoped Part Data Logs

## Context and Problem Statement

Procurement orders pilot parts outside the inspection application. When parts ship, the supplier must provide common shipment information and part-specific quantities and evidence. A physical consignment may contain multiple part numbers and material for multiple PPAPs or build events, while quality records must remain unambiguous by PPAP and build event.

Receipt acknowledgment and workload assignment are also separate responsibilities from inspection execution.

## Decision Drivers

- Capture supplier evidence without duplicating common shipment data.
- Keep PPAP and build-event boundaries explicit.
- Restrict submitted parts to the applicable pilot program.
- Confirm physical receipt before inspection begins.
- Assign work based on receiving-team workload and a target date.
- Preserve traceability for replacement shipments generated by failures.

## Considered Options

### Create one record per physical box

Matches logistics but can combine unrelated PPAP and event contexts.

### Create a complete shipment record for every part number

Keeps part data separate but duplicates common shipment information.

### Create a shared shipment parent with PPAP- and event-scoped part-level children

Separates common logistics data from quality scope while retaining a controlled operational handoff.

## Decision Outcome

Create one pilot shipment parent for the supplier's physical consignment. Record the submitter, shipment date, shipment method, and mandatory tracking reference. A shipment may contain material for more than one PPAP or build event.

Create one child data-log record for each submitted part number and quality context. Relate the data log, rather than the shipment header, to its PPAP and selected pilot build-event context. After the supplier selects the PPAP, filter the build-event choices to those enabled in that PPAP's pilot program. Restrict part selection to the program scope and capture the shipped quantity and applicable pilot data packet files.

Route the submitted shipment and its data logs to the designated inspection-team lead. The team lead acknowledges physical receipt, confirms or records any discrepancy, assigns each part-level inspection activity to an inspector, and sets a target date. The inspection activity then uses ADR-040 to generate unit-level inspections from the received quantity and the active published checklist version for that pilot program.

Replacement shipments created under ADR-045 use the same structure and remain linked to the originating failure and build event.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Common shipment information is entered once.
- Part-level quantities and evidence remain structured and reportable.
- Receipt and assignment are explicit rather than inferred.
- Replacement cycles reuse the same tested workflow.

### Negative

- Suppliers must choose the correct PPAP and event.
- One shipment can contain several quality contexts, increasing child-record and form-filtering complexity.
- Team leads acquire an explicit receipt-and-assignment task.

### Follow-up and Constraints

- Define shipment-method values and receipt-discrepancy behavior.
- Define notification content and assignment permissions.
- Confirm whether shipped quantities can be corrected after submission.
- Define supplier form filtering and portal guidance.
